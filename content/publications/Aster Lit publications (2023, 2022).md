While ["A Chronicle of 9-Dollar Haircuts — These Labors of Love"](https://www.asterlit.org/lacuna-joshuapeter), published in Issue 10 — Winter 2023 of Aster Lit (themed "lacuna"), is nominally 2 standalone pieces, they were intended to complement one another. Each a wistful, colorful vignette occurring somewhere in and around my current hometown—Houston—these poems are an ode to the unremarkable, diversity, and the simple joys of day-to-day life. "A Chronicle of $9 Haircuts" is a tribute to the short-statured, impeccably groomed Peruvian hairstylist who had cut my hair for over a decade since we moved to Texas, while "These Labors of Love" champions the Cambodian proprietor of my favorite local donut/kolache shop, which I religiously visited when I was a barista before my 5:30 AM opening shifts at the coffeeshop. For me these poems were a stylistic exercise in brevity, and as somebody whose writing can tend verbose, I relished the challenge of writing something short and accessible. 

My prose piece, ["cloud factories"](https://www.asterlit.org/fall2022/joshua-peter), published in Issue 7 — Fall 2022 of Aster Lit (themed "remembrance"), reaches further back into my past. "cloud factories" is a love story that never happened, drawing on my early 2000s upbringing in a small, majority-white suburb of Philadelphia. Inspired by Flower Face's [titular song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?app=desktop&v=uK9qkkmlKOE)and informed by the sights, smells, and sounds of the 8 years I lived in Pennsylvania, "cloud factories" remains one of the most depressing things I have ever written—and the first to ever receive recognition from any (English-language) literary journal, receiving an honorable mention for Aster Lit's Starlit Award for prose. It aims to capture the almost surreal cocktail of nostalgia, melancholy, and liminality I felt at having listened to the song of the same name for the first time. Even though it is a work of fiction, the narrative and characters of "cloud factories" remain vibrant in my mind, as if a memory of a life I could've lived.
