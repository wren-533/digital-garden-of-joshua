<img src="/Attachments/AATSP.png" alt="AATSP Logo" style="float: right; margin-left: 1rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; margin-top: 0rem; width: 300px;" /> At the behest of my AP Spanish IV/V instructor (love you Dra. Torres!) who believed in my abilities much more than I did myself, I submitted to The American Association of Teachers of Spanish & Portuguese (AATSP) student publication Albricias. I was a high school member of la Sociedad Honoraria Hispánica (SHH, affiliated with AATSP) at the time and had never written Spanish-language poetry prior. Writing in a language I could only understand in a scholastic capacity was daunting but made the process of incorporating literary devices that much more novel and exciting. By my junior year, I had the privilege of being published in 2 issues of Albricias: "Canción de Las Luciérnagas" ("Song of the Fireflies", [Fall 2021](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.aatsp.org/resource/resmgr/albricias/final_pdfs/Nov_2021_Albricias_web_versi.pdf)) and "Río, un Poema" ("River, a Poem", [Spring 2022](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.aatsp.org/resource/resmgr/albricias/final_pdfs/Albricias_Mar_2022_Final_for.pdf)). The former is a reflection on childhood wonder, while the latter is told from the point of view of an Aztec elder to a child and recounts the subjugation of *la conquista*.
