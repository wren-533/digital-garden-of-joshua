*After George Ella Lyon's poem "Where I'm From".*
# I. son
i am my mother's boy, who knows which teflon pans can take the abrasive green of a scotch-brite sponge / whose face was spared the potent accutane but not the persistent whiteheads;

mamma, sage and skeptic, who tells me things like "to bury a parent is an honor, but to bury a child is a curse," / if such things are to believed, mamma holds the esteem and privilege of a queen because she put both parents six feet under before she could finish her roaring 20s / but also because she planted her roots firm / coaxed a flourishing garden kingdom / from the scorched plains of her own fragile fig-heart; 

i am my father's son who is enamored with knowing / my brain ever-hungry for knowledge / my father who phones colleagues on drives when there is nothing to say / or listens to npr and old malayalam songs, fuzzy and wailing, when the gap between us feels too far to bridge;

dada, whose hair-trigger temper i am said to have inherited / only he seethes in stoic solemnity / and i decompose, curdle and sour into bitter words i'm not sure i don't mean / dada who, if cataclysm ensued and the plane was going down / would loop the elastics of oxygen masks behind the ears of others before his own / reckless selflessness in everything;

dada says that in his eyes, i am still the wrinkled, delicate bundle of flesh he took home, a child of winter born on the cusp of spring / mamma says i am the first child she has ever held and the first child she has ever loved;

the tectonics of arguments: convergence with dada brings only the buckling of earth, the creation of new ridges until we are separate continents; 

subsidence with mamma, where deceit leads to a sinking and my rebellion is made into magma once more, simmering dormant beneath the surface;

we are our roughest at these invisible fault lines / jagged as if we were styrofoam cut by a kindergartener kitted with an x-acto knife for a diorama / there are unseen depths to these rifts: to the oceans contained in mamma’s pecan praline eyes / the inflection of dada’s sunday morning baritone / but i have made peace with the fact that one can never truly know another: even those who gave them life;

for every time i have shaken ground with raised voice / in hopes of knowing you better, i atone with an earnest *i’m sorry* / in a tongue i cannot claim to know, something salient is gained in translation: *ennodu kshamikku*[^1]—tolerate me / know i do not mean the words i say / that these lips only ever intend love: *njan snehikunnu*[^2]—i am loving you / because it is a choice i make every day; and i will choose love unconditionally / until the stories of your lives are all but punctuated simply, indefinitely, with two terrible semicolons; i will celebrate the people i knew you to be in my remembrance. 
# II. material love
i tell you that love is as material as it is immaterial:

i tell you that love is the sore corners of our mouths / marred by over-toasted bread / foil-wrapped peanut butter, banana, and jelly sandwiches clutched in the cold hands of my family / seated in a dusty gold nissan minivan at 6:30 in the morning / dressed in our sunday best on the way to church in autumn / chilly but aflame with her vivid rusting regalia;

i'm from sonic chili cheese anything on thursday school nights / proffering tots for sip-bites of my sister’s viscous ice cream shakes / and greasy fistfuls of arby's curly fries clenched between tiny fingers / as we watched planes take off from the trunk of our car: flying, flying, flying: yaw, pitch, roll like badminton birdies eclipsing crayola-blue skies / like sly fireflies evading mason jar capture / zipping across sleepy dimming landscapes;

i'm from crumpled bounce dryer sheets redolent with soapy softener / the heady pine-sol (that mamma grew to hate) wet on tiles; i'm from knees skinned on bus stop pavement / *kiss it better, dust it off* / balmy neosporin applicators clenched between dada’s fingertips: these keloid trinkets of my childhood;

i'm from smuggling aunt jemima syrup / under the dining table with the matte finish that raised the hairs on my arms when scratched / to sip in clandestine corn syrup paradise;

i'm from the spice and burn of liquor miniatures on *appacha’s*[^3] breath / the scent of *ethakka appam*[^4] frying on the deck / turmeric-tinted oil clinging to paper towels; honeysuckle nectar sticky and foaming effervescent on my lips in autumn / cardamom-laced *chaya*[^5], chicory-embittered *kaapi*[^6] / *ammachi*[^7] pulling milk between scalding stainless steel pitchers at circus heights / frothy, burbling violently…

i'm from fiddling with shoelaces for an eternity / because my clumsy fingers didn't have the dexterity / to coax the bunny around the tree and into its den / till mamma showed me that there wasn’t a wrong way: that the garden path / the path less taken / could be just as valid;

i'm from the hodgepodge grape-eyed carrot-nosed lump of snow we christened a snowman / in the all-blanketing white of philadelphia winters; from mamma having us stuff loose change / into cardboard coin rolls weeks before christmas / so that santa would have a down payment for our presents / even when we lived paycheck to paycheck; 

i am rooted in pennsylvania piedmont, fertile with minerality / in the strokes of my father tracing the *kurishu*[^8] on my forehead after a nightmare / but transient all the same as lamplight fire / dancing on houston bayous in a midspring's twilight;

when i pass / let my tongue taste once again / a rita’s misto shake after the chlorinated tang of a cyan ymca swimming pool / and as you lay me down / soundtrack the funeral with kidz bop 2012 and the jessie laugh track / anoint my body with every flavor of neon / adorn it with my rainbow loom treasures / mark the grave with a fuse-bead plaque that reads:

[^1]: ennodu kshamikku (എന്നോട് ക്ഷമിക്കൂ), lit. “tolerate me”, an apology
[^2]:njan snehikunnu (ഞാൻ സ്നേഹിക്കുന്നു), lit. “I am loving \[you\]”, an expression of affection
[^3]:appacha (അപ്പച്ച), grandfather
[^4]:ethakka appam (ഏത്തക്ക അപ്പം), a sweet plantain fritter snack
[^5]:chaya (ചായ), malayalam for chai
[^6]:kaapi (കോഫി), coffee
[^7]:ammachi (അമ്മച്ചി), grandmother
[^8]:kurishu (കുരിശ്), sign of the cross
