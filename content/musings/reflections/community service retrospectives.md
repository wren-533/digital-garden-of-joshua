>[!Abstract] My relationship with volunteering
>Volunteering is a great passion of mine that has fallen by the wayside since I graduated high school and took on additional responsibilities. I have fond memories of volunteering with great organizations like the Loaves & Fishes Soup Kitchen and the Rosenberg Railroad Museum and cherish the way they helped me expand my worldview and get to know people from different walks of life. It was a feeling I sorely missed. 
>
>When a mutual friend of friends, Jean-Paul, approached me last fall about joining [[🙣 about me|ASME-UH's]] community service committee (which he chaired), I hesitantly accepted, fearful that I would not have the time to commit. While I may not have been as involved as I would've liked, I am happy to say that over the course of my junior year, I was able to show out to several opportunities to help better the world around me. Serving alongside him and my good friends/fellow community service assistants, Vincent and Maria, and helping out those in need proved to be so gratifying. Check out some of the highlights below!

# armand bayou nature center (11.1.225)
Perhaps the weirdest and most unique volunteering opportunity I have had to date. This was my first time in Pasadena and it was certainly a time to remember: the Armand Bayou Nature Center is one the largest wilderness reserve in the state of Texas at over 4000 acres, and boasts a historic sugarcane field that is tended to by hand and used to demonstrate the equipment (preserved on site) used to process the crop. 

Sugarcane was a very important cash crop in the coastal prairie region back in the day, so it was fascinating to learn more about it—from how it grows to how sugar is made from it to its historical significance to the people who worked with and around it. We (Maria, Jean-Paul, and myself) were tasked with cutting down and stripping stalks of sugarcane to be processed for a fall festival-type event. 

It was a cold and nippy, dewy fall morning pleasantly uncharacteristic of Houston in November and that made the whole experience that much better. Maria and I hit up Koffeteria in East Downtown after for some scrumptious pastries: beef phở kolaches and French onion soup danish. It was the perfect way to finish the morning.
