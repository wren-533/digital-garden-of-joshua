To explain my perspective on epistemic disclosure, I'd do well to formally define it for you as I understand it. 

The adjective *epistemic* refers to knowledge or knowing, whereas *disclosure*, in this case, refers to a disclaimer. Together, these words can be taken to mean a comment on the credibility of a piece of knowledge: *where*—*how*—was it obtained? What other knowledge informed it (because no idea, no matter how novel, is totally original)? What qualifications (or lack thereof) do I have to speak on the topic this knowledge concerns?

Perhaps it is a symptom of possessing an engineering mind, but I am nothing if not rigorous with my nomenclature systems: on the various pages on this site, you'll see one of the following tags, in keeping with the ethos of "digital gardens":

#sprout – Fledgling notes; ideas/comments I've jotted down quickly but have yet to expand on and link to other notes.
#sapling – Adolescent notes; ideas/comments that have matured and grown into self-determined pieces of knowledge with links to other notes.
#tree – Matured notes; robust ideas/comments that have been developed to a high degree with multiple links to other notes.

#deciduous – A modifier signifying a note that I actively expect to edit; a work-in-progress or draft that I've published in incomplete form. 
#evergreen – A modifier signifying a note that I consider archival; this does not exempt it from future changes but means that I can treat it as a useful reference.

#fertilized – A modifier signifying a note with explicit references which will be mentioned at the end. 
#fruited – A modifier signifying a note for which I have epistemic confidence; content that I believe is high-quality, mostly free from error, and/or well-backed.
#fallowed – A modifier signifying a note for which I lack epistemic confidence; content that I believe is of questionable quality, particularly error-prone, and/or maybe not substantiated.
