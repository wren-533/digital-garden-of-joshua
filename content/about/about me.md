When I hear the word engineering, one of the first things that comes to mind is quality: our bottom line as problem-solvers and innovators is noticing small things and designing with them in mind—ethically, cost-consciously and elegantly. 

Hi, my name is Joshua Peter and quality is something I think about often. I am a senior/accelerated master's mechanical engineering student at the University of Houston, and provisional Engineer-in-Training (EIT). Experiences like working as a barista in specialty coffee for 3.5 years (2 of which in management) and serving as Vice President Operations for ASME-UH have shaped within me the strong attention to detail demanded of an engineer.

I currently intern for Schweitzer Engineering Laboratories (SEL), an electrical engineering company and the progenitor of the modern digital microprocessor-based relay, where I support civil/structural design for electrical substations. Working in power systems and pursuing our mission at SEL—to make electric power safer, more reliable, and more economical—has taught me how integral energy is to our day-to-day lives. This role has shown me what it means to synergize within a multidisciplinary team to create impact for stakeholders, both internal and external. 

In my mind, the pursuit of quality is ultimately an optimization problem: what is the ceiling to our achievement given a scarcity of resources? Since my time as a barista, I have looked to two Japanese principles for answers: *kaizen*, or working in the spirit of continuous, incremental improvement, and *omotenashi*, or the practice of profound, unconditional hospitality. *Lean* as a methodology is sterile and ineffective without sincerity and genuine care for our colleagues and customers. 

I rely on these guiding principles daily, whether it be as a student, an employee, a servant leader, a creative, or simply a human being. I aspire to contribute to the prestige and credibility of the engineering profession both in the classroom and the workplace.

Outside of engineering, you can find me hunting down new $\rm{\LaTeX}$ packages on CTAN to mess around with, reading and writing the occasional poem, singing/playing piano, cuddling with my dogs, and almost always brewing or drinking something caffeinated. I also thoroughly enjoy community service, dip-pen calligraphy, cooking, Oxford commas, and most recently—being a gym denizen.
