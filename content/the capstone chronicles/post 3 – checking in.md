# state of the project
Hi! It’s been some time since Team Wind Waker’s last post. Below are updated statements of work and goals, as priorities have been reassessed over the course of the semester.

## statement of work
Team Wind Waker will perform a detailed literature review, develop functional specifications, conduct CFD and FEA analysis on components, and ultimately fabricate two VAWTs: one in the unpitched H-Darrieus configuration (intended to be a baseline upon which to measure improvement in efficiency) and a pitched helical VAWT—the actual product. While both will be 1:1 scale prototypes, only the latter will serve as the formal design solution required by MECE 4340/4341. Team Wind Waker will fix parameters such as blade helicity, number, material, and airfoil shape at those values/configurations deemed optimal by current literature, performing CFD analysis on CAD-generated VAWT models that have had their blade pitch parametrically modified so as to determine the pitch angle that results in the highest percent improvement in power coefficient $Cp$ relative to the control H-Darrieus turbine. Validation will be performed in the ATLAS lab's wind tunnel at wind conditions characteristic of Houston, Texas.

## statement of goals
Team Wind Waker aspires for the pitched helical VAWT design solution to achieve a minimum 10% increase in power coefficient $Cp$ relative to the team-contrived unpitched control H-Darrieus turbine and a prospective generating capacity of 1 kW at an ideal sustained wind speed of 8 m/s. Team Wind Waker envisions this project as a step towards the democratization/decentralization of the means of energy production and making renewable energy production a more attractive, cost-effective, choice for consumers.

Additionally, Miguel began preparations for Team Wind Waker’s impending Design Review presentation by developing a design matrix for the permanent magnet generator (PMG), which will be utilized to convert the incident wind energy into electric power. Considering the constraints previously defined in Milestone I, PMGs were deemed optimal for their high efficiency, power density, reduced maintenance, market availability, reliability, and durability. Particular attention was given to finding a reliable product from a reputable vendor, in line with the project vision of democratizing renewable energy. A maximum rated output of 100 W with a target weight of < 10 lbs was found to be the most desirable for Team Wind Waker’s application.

Finally, Arturo developed a baseline CAD model in SolidWorks for an unpitched helical VAWT, which will be modified accordingly as a final pitch angle is decided. This model will be updated to reflect the finalized blade material/method of fabrication: PETG-CF, a 3D printing-compatible composite filament comprised of carbon fibers embedded in a PETG (glycol‑modified polyethylene terephthalate) matrix. Blade rotor height and diameter have been selected in such a way that blades can be printed as single, continuous parts within the spatial constraints of the available 3D printer, whenever Team Wind Waker chooses to do so.

# where we're going (work period of nov. 15–nov. 29)
From a preliminary design standpoint, Team Wind Waker aims to finalize the design of the pitched, helical VAWT within Q-Blade—namely the blade design, wind field, and turbine—using the controller library and structural data. Along with this, Q-Blade simulations will be conducted to generate power coefficient vs. tip speed ratio curves. Completion of these tasks will mark the end of Milestone II and will allow the team to transition into optimization-oriented aerodynamic performance simulations, using Q-Blade as the primary CFD software. 

It is the team’s hope that the current MATLAB script will be refined to the point where power and torque coefficient curves generated from the model agree well with the results of analysis on Q-Blade. This will confirm that the mechanics motivating design choices (double-multiple streamtube theory) matches software output. 

Besides performance analysis, the team also wants to begin work on assessing the structural integrity of the design solution. In other words, will the VAWT fail under maximum wind loadings as specified in the Table of Major Project Constraints? Will generated angular velocities exceed rated values for the chosen PMG? What consideration should be given to fatigue/vibrational loadings?

In terms of course progression, Team Wind Waker will continue preparation for Design Review presentations, furnishing comparisons of preliminary design concepts with the intention of convincing stakeholders that the solution is the best solution to the problem. Team Wind Waker will continue biweekly project touchpoints with technical advisors Drs. Huang/Yang, obtaining feedback as more progress is made towards the remainder of the milestones for this semester. 
# obstacles & contingencies
Many of the parameters needed within Q-Blade were not previously defined (camber length, Reynolds number, N-crit), so it was necessary to refer to literature on VAWT design, software documentation, and online help forums. Sometimes, this involved performing hand computations with externally obtained formulas to derive the needed inputs. Additionally, the team had no prior experience using Q-Blade, so using the program to generate simulations has and will be more time-intensive than initially planned.

Another obstacle encountered was with the MATLAB script: the power coefficient vs. tip speed ratio curves yielded maximum $C_p$ values on the order of thousandths, whereas the desired range is > 0.2. This could be attributed to knowledge gaps the team might have with understanding and applying the double-multiple streamtube theory, so plans have been made to troubleshoot with the technical advisors.  

When it comes to project schedule, in order to make sure cresting the learning curve for Q-Blade does not negatively impact the team’s schedule, Team Wind Waker’s Gantt chart has incorporated time buffers between milestones to absorb unforeseen delays. Computational time will not become a bottleneck for the project, as the team has allocated extra days for simulations and aerodynamic performance testing to ensure sufficient time to troubleshoot and validate parameters (especially considering that CFD in Q-Blade is 2-dimensional).
